PRODUCT CONTEXT
Questling is a bilingual (English/Russian) Dark Fantasy language-learning Telegram Mini App and browser demo with a separate Teacher Studio. Students belong to teacher-managed groups with CEFR levels A1–C1. It contains Quick Quests, branching Story Adventures, investigations, puzzles and clues, daily vocabulary/facts, XP, Coins, cosmetic rewards, a shop/inventory, and teacher-authored group-specific assignments. The existing architecture includes Next.js App Router, TypeScript, PostgreSQL/Supabase where configured, protected Teacher Studio, server-verified Telegram authentication, and automated tests. Existing features may be implemented already: audit instead of duplicating or replacing them.

PART 1 — CONTEXTUAL TRANSLATIONS AND HINTS INSIDE EXERCISES
===========================================================
1. Support annotated words AND multiword phrases within questions, answers when pedagogically appropriate, reading passages, character dialogue, stories, puzzles, and daily activities.
2. Tap/click highlighted text to reveal a compact translation popover or mobile bottom sheet. Example: “Find the hidden entrance” -> hidden: “скрытый, спрятанный” and optional “a hidden door — потайная дверь.” The English source should stay visible, without forcing full-page translation.
3. Include optional short definition, example sentence, pronunciation/audio when available, CEFR tag, and contextual explanation. Avoid revealing the correct answer indirectly when the highlighted term is itself the tested answer: teacher/content editor must be able to restrict hints in such cases.
4. Teachers can create/edit/remove annotations in Teacher Studio in built-in and custom activities, using validated text offsets or stable token/phrase anchors. Preserve text formatting, Unicode, punctuation, accessibility and localization. Do not rely on brittle string replacement.
5. Provide optional contextual help for more complex questions, conceptually distinct from translations. The help gives a clue or explanation, NOT a solution. A viewed translation should not reduce XP; contextual help should not automatically penalize students.
6. CEFR-aware defaults: offer more support for A1/A2 than advanced groups but allow teacher overrides. Do not invent translations via unconfigured external services. If no translation exists, show original text normally.
7. Track optionally viewed/saved vocabulary for Part 5; avoid collecting unnecessary personal analytics.
8. Test multiline text, identical repeated terms, overlapping annotations, link/selection interaction, EN/RU switching, keyboard and screen-reader accessibility, and phone widths.

===========================================================
PART 2 — FAIR RANDOMIZATION OF ANSWERS AND GAME ELEMENTS
===========================================================
1. Eliminate predictable “option A is always correct” behavior in all applicable single-choice, multi-choice, daily, story and teacher-created activities.
2. Use a correct unbiased shuffle (e.g., Fisher–Yates) at the creation of each NEW genuine attempt; derive correctness from stable option IDs, never array indexes.
3. Persist an attempt-specific shuffled order or seed server-side so reload, resume, offline reconnect, review and analytics maintain the same display order within an attempt. New attempt => freshly shuffled order. Do not reshuffle mid-attempt or while submitting.
4. Server grades by stable answer IDs and authorized attempt/version; never trusts client-computed correctness or XP.
5. Support multi-select and explain why answers are correct after grading. Shuffle matching pairs and other order-independent items where appropriate. DO NOT randomize tasks where the sequence itself is the educational answer, nor change authored ordered narratives.
6. Teacher Studio setting: automatic shuffle on by default for eligible activity types, teacher override where pedagogically justified. Keep older published quests and saved attempts working through versioned migrations.
7. Add tests proving correct answers appear in varied positions across many fresh attempts, without relying on flaky single randomized samples; test retries, refresh, version changes, accessibility, grading, awards and answer explanation mapping.

CORE LOOP — LINE BY LINE
A. Student watches/listens to a short original video scene with synchronized English subtitle dialogue.
B. Student sees the chosen speaker’s next line and can replay the original line at normal speed or slower (0.75x / 0.5x where pitch support permits), with optional translation/vocabulary popovers.
C. Student explicitly grants microphone access, gets a countdown, and records their own reading/acting of that single line while watching the appropriate scene segment, without hearing overlapping original dialogue when dubbing. Do not capture camera video.
D. Student may listen, delete, retry, accept, then move to next assigned line.
E. After all lines, preview the WHOLE SCENE with student recordings placed at their respective timestamps. Switch instantly between original scene and “My Voice” preview. Show subtitles and progress.
F. Student submits the final performance privately to an authorized teacher and can receive feedback; teacher approval and server-side XP awards follow configured learning rules.

THREE COMPATIBLE ACTIVITY MODES
1. Listen & Repeat: one short line, original -> record -> compare.
2. Voice Over: replace one selected character’s lines with student recordings in the video scene.
3. Roleplay: other characters’ lines play, student performs assigned role at timed cues. If a clip contains multiple speakers, offer character selection; skip it for one speaker.
4. Shadowing Mode: offer beginner-friendly practice where student repeats just after an original (pause-and-repeat) or optionally simultaneously with it. Correctly handle overlapping playback and microphone echo; label it separately from scored dubbing and avoid pretending it is objectively graded.

AUDIO AND VIDEO REALISM
1. Use native HTML5 video, browser MediaRecorder / getUserMedia with feature detection and runtime-supported MIME negotiation. Respect user gestures, Telegram WebView, mic permissions, iOS/Android browser differences, interruptions and cleanup. Never claim a real-device test unless performed.
2. Synchronize recordings with video segment timestamps, ensuring stable replay, seeking, pause/resume, switching tracks and drift handling. Initial v1 can play video + dynamically aligned recorded audio: a fully encoded downloadable dubbed MP4 is NOT a requirement. If no true export pipeline exists, do not display fake export controls.
3. When original dialogue and music/SFX are mixed in one audio track, muting it removes ALL sound in that segment. Do not falsely claim vocals can be removed independently. Optionally allow teacher to upload a separate background-only track or stems to retain ambiance. Do not mandate paid voice separation.
4. Retakes should not result in unlimited permanent cloud files: keep current or limited historical takes per configured retention policy; clean temporary media safely.
5. Display clear loading, permission, recording, upload, failure, unsupported codec and expired-link states; never simulate recordings.
6. Short clips first: recommended ~10–60 seconds. Optimize actual media sizes for free storage and mobile bandwidth. Do not apply old audio-upload 2MB/60s limits to video; establish separate, documented quotas based on real free-tier limits.

TEACHER STUDIO — REAL VIDEO EXERCISE EDITOR
1. Authorized teacher uploads a short licensed/original MP4/WebM (MOV only if codec/transcoding really supported). Validate actual content, MIME, format, size, duration and codec server-side. Secure against unsafe uploads and cross-teacher access. If formats unsupported, reject with clear explanation.
2. Provide nondestructive trim in/out, preview, timeline, manually placed dialogue segments with start/end times, speaker names, optional portraits, English transcript, optional RU translation, term-level glosses, subtitle timing, teacher instructions, student-target lines, optional separate music/ambience track. Do not pretend speech-to-text or diarization automatically works without a tested implementation; manual entry/edit always possible.
3. Teacher sets title, description, A1–C1 level, one or more assigned groups, unlock/deadline, XP, retry/submission allowances, practice modes, subtitle visibility modes (English / English + translation / hidden), teacher-review policy.
4. Draft, validate, preview as student, publish version, assign to groups, edit later without invalidating historical submissions. Integrate as a reusable activity node in the existing branching Story Builder and standalone Quick Quest/daily exercise where appropriate.
5. Teacher can view/listen to recordings, original text, student voice-over scene, optionally compare individual lines, write feedback, use a transparent rubric and approve/request retry. Do not fabricate automated pronunciation scores.
6. Offer choice of roles when multiple characters. Ensure other dialogue remains playable if separate/appropriate source tracks exist; otherwise clearly explain mute limitation.

STUDENT PRACTICE & FEEDBACK
1. Provide line-level original/my-recording A/B switch for instant comparison; original video remains available at all times.
2. Subtitle modes: EN only, EN + RU on demand, no subtitles, with settings appropriate to CEFR level and teacher restrictions.
3. Store student privacy-safe voice progress to let student compare today's recording with earlier approved/submitted recordings if they opt in and within defined retention limits. Protect recordings and let authorized students delete where allowed; never share them publicly by default.
4. Teacher feedback is authoritative for graded performance; optional self-review checklist may address articulation, pacing, stress and rhythm without fabricated percentages.
5. If a submitted dubbing mission advances the story, unlock next clue/location/branch and optional cosmetics via existing server-authorized achievement flow. Do not penalize microphone hardware quality or a student's accent with arbitrary scoring.

SECURE MEDIA AND YOUTH PRIVACY
1. Private media storage for teacher video and student voice. Server-authorized access only; short-lived media URLs where necessary. Enforce tenant/group/role-based access on every operation.
2. Explicit microphone permission; no background recording. Proper visibility and deletion, data export and retention policies for minors. Follow applicable child protection/privacy requirements without claiming legal compliance solely from code.
3. Require teacher confirmation of media rights; use original or appropriately licensed sample clips. No downloading copyrighted films from YouTube, bypassing DRM or scraping protected sources.
4. Document free-tier storage/egress/processing constraints. Do not put binary videos in PostgreSQL or rely on persistent Vercel serverless disk. Avoid heavyweight serverless transcoding; select a practical free-compatible upload/processing architecture or clearly document a constrained MVP without fake support.
5. Student rewards tied to assignment submission/review via server-side idempotent records; prevent re-upload XP farming, unauthorized submissions and tampering with client-provided scores.

PART 5 — PERSONAL VOCABULARY JOURNAL & SPACED REPETITION
===========================================================
1. Let students save highlighted words/phrases encountered during quests, daily word, dialogue and subtitles into a personal vocabulary journal. Show term, RU meaning, English example, pronunciation/audio when available, quest/source, date saved and CEFR level.
2. Support search/filter, duplicate merging (respect multiple senses), favorites and optional mark-known; protect per-student records from unauthorized access.
3. Implement genuine spaced repetition (e.g., SM-2-like or simple transparent interval scheduling based on recall quality) with daily review mini-quizzes. Distinguish review cards from main quest grading and never leak answers before the learner attempts them.
4. Generate practice from existing teacher-reviewed words/content rather than inventing unsupported translations. Use the existing XP anti-farming rules for review rewards.
5. Teacher visibility, if appropriate, should be group-limited and focus on aggregate progress / difficult words rather than exposing unnecessary private data.
6. Integrate gentle, non-punitive review reminders into existing daily quest loop. No compulsive streak pressure or punitive loss of earned rewards.

===========================================================

===========================================================
PART 7 — PERSONALIZED RECOMMENDATIONS BASED ON LEARNING ERRORS
===========================================================
1. Tag teacher-authored and built-in questions with learning objectives, vocabulary units, grammar themes, CEFR levels and exercise type.
2. On valid submissions, record minimally necessary topic-level performance. Identify patterns such as repeated errors in Past Simple or a set of vocabulary words; be careful with very small sample sizes.
3. Recommend focused practice and spaced-repetition activities using a deterministic, explainable ranking algorithm without mandatory paid AI. Example: “Review Past Simple: 3 errors in your last 5 related exercises.” Only show numbers actually supported by data.
4. Teacher Studio analytics should summarize learning gaps by authorized group and suggest which tasks to assign. Protect access to individual results and avoid ranking children against each other by personal weaknesses.
5. Recommendations never block story progression or override teacher assignments; teacher may disable/adjust them. Test data sufficiency, cold start, multi-group membership and topic tagging consistency.
